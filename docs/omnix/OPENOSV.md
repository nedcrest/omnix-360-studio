# OpenOSV — what we can take

The user asked on 2026-10-23: "look at OpenOSV on GitHub, maybe something
there applies to us". Researched at commit `d1c657a` (v0.6.3, 2026-10-13) of
https://github.com/Kemerd/OpenOSV. It is a C++20/CUDA plug-in that opens
`.OSV` files in Premiere, Resolve and VEGAS.

Licence: Apache-2.0 with a NOTICE file. Our crate is MIT OR Apache-2.0.
- **Code translated from them** becomes Apache-only. It needs their NOTICE
  and changed-file marks, and the MIT option doesn't cover it.
- **Algorithms reimplemented from their docs** carry no such obligation.
  Credit them anyway.
- **Their `.cube` LUTs** carry no DJI data. NOTICE says each file is
  generated by their own fitted models, so they can be bundled with
  attribution. That is their legal position.

How to read the numbers: almost every stitch measurement comes from **one
65-frame clip** (camera on an aircraft wing, ND filters), and a few from
two car drives. None are from selfie-stick footage like ours. They also
decode their own calibration instead of DJI's rung-11 ±6.84° blend. Read the
stitch numbers as ideas to measure here, not results.

## File facts (gap 24)

- **`.LRF` timing drifts on clips with dropped frames** (night, high ISO).
  The LRF's time table writes 60 ms gaps as 80 ms: 3.1 s too long over
  326 s. The `.OSV` follows the per-frame capture timestamps in
  `FrameMeta.1`. Our proxy playback may drift on such clips. Their rule:
  frame index = sample index = `djmd` index, placed by capture timestamp.
- **6K lens tracks (3000 px) are the central 3776 px of the 3840
  calibration**, scale 0.794492, not 3000/3840. A plain scale misaligns the
  seam by 4.25° (their claim). 8K is 1.0. We take `calib_to_picture` from
  telemetry-parser, so check what it gives on a 6K clip. For 4K and 1920 px
  (our 100 fps PR #5 uses 0.5) they derive it from `digital_focal_length`
  and call it unverified.
- **Per-frame metadata we don't read:** ISO, shutter, white balance in K,
  sensor temperature. Per clip: `sensor_readout_time` ≈ 17 282 160, which
  as ns is 17.28 ms against our measured 18.3 ms. Useful for the Info panel
  and a per-clip rolling-shutter readout.
- Not in the file: GPS (empty messages), spatial or ambisonic sound (one
  stereo AAC track), an ND flag.
- **DJI Studio bends its picture** with an extra "de-distortion" warp that
  OpenOSV doesn't reproduce, and nor do we. At the user's example (FOV 84.1,
  correction 0.49) its weight is about 1, so "same numbers, slightly
  different picture" against DJI Studio is expected. Worth a line in the
  guide.

## Not worth it for us

- **Carved-seam DP.** This is the optimal seam path we tried and dropped.
  Their own conclusion matches ours: it is seam selection, and flow can't fix
  it.
- **Optical-flow parallax grid.** Large to port, and it can't measure the
  thin overlap beside the stick.
- **Per-clip median hold of the seam.** Their Auto mode follows the scene on
  every car clip, and handheld footage would too. Our temporal term already
  gets 0.011°/frame.
- **Sun-ghost fitting.** Large, and solves none of our problems.
- Their decode path: CUDA-native, NVDEC into their own context. It doesn't
  carry over to wgpu. Their GOP-aware cache and decode-ahead policy is an
  idea for upstream's zero-copy item.
- Avata 360: no footage.

## Testing ideas to copy

- Golden values from an independent float64 Python reference that reads the
  constants from the Rust source, so the reference and the code can't drift.
- Generated files (a bundled LUT) regenerated in a test and compared byte
  for byte.
- Tiny synthetic MP4s from a stdlib script: truncated moov, lost IDR,
  variable frame rate.
- Hostile inputs (NaN, infinities, 1e6) in colour and geometry tests.
